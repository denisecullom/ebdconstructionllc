What does a whole-home renovation include?

A whole-home renovation updates most or all of the house at once instead of one room every few years. With EBD Construction it can include:

  1. Layout changes. Removing walls to open the kitchen to the living room, widening doorways and adding support beams where walls come out.
  2. The kitchen. New cabinets, countertops, an island, lighting and appliances. See the kitchen remodeling guide for details.
  3. Bathrooms. Walk-in showers, tubs, tile and vanities. See the bathroom remodeling guide.
  4. Floors throughout. Hardwood or luxury vinyl plank run through the main level so rooms flow together.
  5. Trim, doors and stairs. Baseboards, casing, columns, coffered details, interior doors and stair railings.
  6. Paint and lighting. Fresh paint inside, recessed lights and new fixtures in every room.
  7. Outdoor living and exterior. Screened porches, decks, siding, paint and curb appeal. See the deck and porch guide.
  8. The work behind the walls. Updated electrical, plumbing and framing, handled by the same crew.

Not every house needs everything on this list. Many whole-home projects focus on the main living level: kitchen, living and dining rooms, floors and trim. Tell Eric what matters most during your free consultation and the plan will be built around it.

How much does a whole-home renovation cost in Gainesville, GA?

The cost of a whole-home renovation in Gainesville depends mostly on four things: the size of the house, how many rooms you update, whether walls or plumbing move, and the finishes you choose.

Here's a general guide to what whole-home renovations typically cost in the Gainesville area. It isn't a quote; your price depends on your house and your choices.

Cosmetic whole-home refreshPaint, new floors, trim, lighting, hardware and fixtures; same layout$7,000 to $50,000

Full whole-home remodelKitchen and all baths, walls removed or moved, new floors, trim, lighting and updated systems$40,000 to $250,000
Down-to-the-studs renovationEntire house gutted and rebuilt inside, often with layout, porch or exterior changes$80,000+

The fastest way to get a real number is a free on-site consultation. EBD works within your budget and will tell you up front where your money goes furthest. If the full list is more than you want to spend at once, Eric can phase the work so the most important rooms come first.

Tip: Doing several rooms at once usually costs less than doing them one at a time. Demolition, permits, floors and paint are done in one pass, and you only live through one construction project instead of three.

How long does a whole-home renovation take?

Most whole-home renovations in the Gainesville area take about 4 to 8 weeks for a cosmetic refresh, 2 to 4 months for a main-level renovation and 4 to 8 months for a full or down-to-the-studs remodel. The biggest factors are how much structural work is involved, inspection timing, and lead times on cabinets, windows and custom items, which are ordered early so they arrive when the crew needs them.

A typical EBD whole-home project follows these steps:

  1. Free consultation. Eric walks the whole house with you, listens to what you want and notes what's behind the walls that may need attention.
  2. Plan, priorities and estimate. You get one plan for the whole house, a written estimate and a timeline, with options to phase the work if you'd like.
  3. Selections and ordering. You choose cabinets, counters, tile, flooring, trim, fixtures and lighting. Long-lead items are ordered first.
  4. Permits. Eric pulls every permit himself, with the City of Gainesville or Hall County depending on where you live.
  5. Demolition and structure. Walls come out, beams go in, and framing is updated.
  6. Rough-ins and inspections. Plumbing, electrical and ventilation are updated and inspected before the walls close.
  7. Drywall, floors and finishes. Drywall, cabinets, counters, tile, flooring, trim and paint go in room by room.
  8. Final walkthrough. You walk the finished house with Eric, and anything that isn't right gets fixed.

Can I live in my house during a whole-home renovation?

Often, yes, at least for part of it. For a cosmetic refresh or a main-level project, most families stay home while the crew works through the house in stages, with a temporary kitchen set up in another room. For a down-to-the-studs remodel, or when the only kitchen and bathroom are torn out at the same time, staying with family or renting nearby for a few weeks makes life much easier. Eric will plan the order of work around how you live, so you always know which rooms are usable and when.

Do I need a permit for a whole-home renovation in Gainesville or Hall County?

Almost always, yes. A whole-home renovation usually includes structural, plumbing or electrical work, such as removing a wall, moving fixtures, adding circuits or replacing windows, and that needs a building permit and inspections. Paint, flooring and trim on their own usually do not. Homes inside Gainesville city limits are permitted through the City of Gainesville; homes in unincorporated Hall County go through Hall County. Flowery Branch, Oakwood and Buford each have their own process. EBD Construction pulls all required permits itself as part of the project, so you don't have to.

Should I renovate my house or move?

If you like your neighborhood, your lot and your schools, renovating usually gets you more of what you want for the money. Selling and buying in Hall County means agent commissions, closing costs and moving expenses, which can add up to 8 to 10 percent of the home's price before you change a thing, and the next house will have its own list of projects. Moving makes more sense when you need much more space than the lot allows or want a different location. During the consultation Eric can tell you what your wish list would take in your current house, so you can compare.

Which rooms should I renovate first?

If you're phasing the work, start with anything structural or behind the walls: wall removal, plumbing, electrical and windows. Next come the kitchen and main living areas, because they get the most use and add the most value. Bathrooms, bedrooms and outdoor spaces can follow. Floors are best done after the messy work, and paint goes last. Planning the whole house up front, even if you build it in phases, keeps you from tearing out new work later.

Whole-home renovation FAQs

What's the difference between a whole-home renovation and a remodel?

People use the words loosely. A remodel usually means updating one room or area, like a kitchen or a bath. A whole-home renovation updates most or all of the house under one plan and one schedule, often including layout changes, floors and trim throughout.

Can I renovate my whole house in phases?

Yes. Many Gainesville homeowners plan the whole house at once and then build it in two or three phases to spread out the cost. Doing the structural and behind-the-walls work first keeps later phases from undoing earlier ones.

Can you take out a load-bearing wall?

Usually, yes. A load-bearing wall can be removed when a properly sized beam and supports carry the load instead. EBD handles the framing and beam work and pulls the permit, so the work is inspected.

Do you help with design, or do I need an architect?

EBD provides full design services and start-to-finish project management, so most whole-home renovations do not need a separate designer. Larger structural changes or additions may need engineered plans, which EBD can coordinate. If you already work with a designer or architect, EBD can build from their plans.

Is a whole-home renovation worth it on an older home?

Often, yes. Many older homes in Gainesville and around Lake Lanier sit on great lots in established neighborhoods. Updating the kitchen, baths, floors and layout can give you a house that feels new without giving up the location.

Can you add space during a whole-home renovation?

Yes. EBD builds room additions, screened porches and decks, and can include them in the same plan so the new space matches the rest of the house.
