Home Inspection Repairs in Gainesville, GA: What They Cost and What to Fix

What the items on a typical Hall County inspection report cost to fix, which ones matter most, repairs vs. closing credits, and how to get them done before closing, for buyers and sellers in Gainesville, Hall County and around Lake Lanier.

By Eric Bubniak, owner of EBD Construction LLC · November 12, 2026

Quick answer: For most homes in Gainesville and Hall County, a typical home inspection repair list of small items, like rotten trim, GFCI outlets, loose railings, caulking and deck flashing, runs about $1,500 to $10,000. Which items matter most?

  • Safety first. Loose railings, rotten deck framing, missing GFCIs, electrical hazards and missing detectors.
  • Water next. Roof leaks, flashing, gutters, grading and plumbing leaks cause the most damage if left alone.
  • Structure. Rotten sill plates, sagging beams or soft floors.
  • Lender and appraiser items. Peeling paint on older homes, missing handrails, roof condition and anything unsafe can hold up FHA and VA loans.
  • Cosmetic items last. Worn carpet, dated fixtures and small drywall cracks are usually not worth negotiating over.

Repairs or a closing credit?

Sellers often repair safety items and anything a lender will flag, because those can hold up the loan. For bigger items, like a roof near the end of its life, a price reduction or closing credit is often simpler and lets the buyer pick their own contractor. Buyers who take a credit should get a written estimate first so the number covers the real cost. Your real estate agent can advise on what your contract allows.

How long do I have?

In a typical Georgia purchase contract, inspections and repair requests happen during the due diligence period, which the buyer and seller agree on and is often about 7 to 14 days. Repairs are usually finished before closing, often 2 to 4 weeks later. Send us the inspection report and photos as soon as you have them so Eric can walk the house and price each item in writing.

Selling soon? Fix it before the inspection

Fixing obvious problems before you list, like rot, peeling paint, leaks and broken railings, helps a house sell faster and avoids surprises in the buyer's inspection. A pre-listing refresh typically costs about $3,000 to $20,000 and takes one to four weeks. See our remodeling ROI guide for which updates pay back.

Do repairs need permits and receipts?

Small repairs like trim, caulking, outlets and railings usually don't need permits. Roof structure, an electrical panel, a water heater or structural framing usually do. Buyers often ask for receipts, photos and permit records at closing, so use a licensed, insured contractor. Eric pulls all permits himself; see our building permits guide.

Home inspection repair FAQs

What are the most common home inspection problems in Georgia?

In North Georgia, the most common items are wood rot on trim, fascia and door frames, roof wear and flashing leaks, deck ledger and railing problems, missing GFCI outlets, crawl space moisture, water heater issues, gutters and grading that send water toward the house, and missing smoke or carbon monoxide detectors.

Should a seller make repairs or give a credit?

It depends on the item and the timeline. Safety items and anything a lender or appraiser will flag, like rotten wood, missing handrails or roof leaks, are often best repaired before closing. Cosmetic items and big-ticket upgrades are often handled as a price reduction or closing credit so the buyer can choose their own contractor. Your real estate agent can advise on your contract.

How long do I have to negotiate repairs in Georgia?

In a typical Georgia purchase contract, inspections and repair requests happen during the due diligence period, which the buyer and seller agree on and is often about 7 to 14 days. Repairs are usually finished before closing, often 2 to 4 weeks later. Your agent and contract set the exact dates.

Do inspection repairs need permits?

Small repairs like trim, caulking, outlets and railings usually don't. Replacing a roof structure, an electrical panel, a water heater or structural framing usually does. Buyers often ask for receipts and permit records, so use a licensed contractor. Eric pulls all permits himself.

Can a contractor give me an estimate from the inspection report?

Yes. Send us the report and photos, and Eric can walk the house, price each item in writing and tell you which ones are urgent. Buyers and sellers both use a written estimate to negotiate.

Should I fix things before listing my house?

Fixing obvious problems before listing, like rot, peeling paint, leaks and broken railings, helps a house sell faster and avoids surprises in the buyer's inspection. A pre-listing refresh typically costs about $3,000 to $20,000 and takes one to four weeks.
