How do I know if a wall is load-bearing?

A load-bearing wall holds up part of the house above it: ceiling joists, a second floor or the roof. A few clues point to one:

  • The wall runs perpendicular to the ceiling joists (you can check in the attic).
  • It sits near the middle of the house, often under the roof's ridge.
  • There's another wall, a beam or a post directly above or below it, such as a girder in the basement or crawl space.
  • It's on the first floor of a two-story house under rooms upstairs.

These are only clues. Many older Gainesville homes have been remodeled before, and walls don't always do what they appear to do. The reliable answer comes from looking at what's actually sitting on the wall, which Eric does during the free consultation.

Can a load-bearing wall be removed?

Usually, yes. When a load-bearing wall comes out, a beam takes over its job, and posts at each end carry the weight down to the foundation. Here's how EBD does it:

  1. Temporary walls go up on one or both sides to hold the ceiling while the wall comes out.
  2. The wall is removed and any wiring, plumbing or ductwork inside it is rerouted.
  3. The beam is installed, either dropped below the ceiling or set flush into it, with posts and footings sized for the load.
  4. The work is inspected under the building permit Eric pulls.
  5. Everything is finished: drywall, ceiling and floor patching, trim and paint, so the opening looks like it was always there.

How long does it take to remove a wall?

Removing a non-load-bearing wall usually takes about 2 to 5 days, including patching and paint. A load-bearing wall with a new beam usually takes 1 to 3 weeks, depending on the span, inspection timing and how much floor and ceiling work follows. When the wall comes out as part of a kitchen or whole-home remodel, it's scheduled into that project so the floors, drywall and paint are done once.

Do I need a permit to remove a wall in Gainesville or Hall County?

For a load-bearing wall, yes. Removing a wall that carries part of the house is structural work and needs a building permit and inspection. Moving plumbing, wiring or ductwork that runs through a wall usually needs a permit too. Homes inside Gainesville city limits are permitted through the City of Gainesville; homes in unincorporated Hall County go through Hall County. Flowery Branch, Oakwood and Buford each have their own process. EBD Construction pulls all required permits itself as part of the project, so you don't have to.

Wall removal and open floor plan FAQs

Can I remove a wall that has plumbing or wiring in it?

Usually, yes. Wiring is often easy to reroute. Plumbing vents and drains take more planning and add cost, and ductwork may need to move into the ceiling or a nearby wall. Eric checks what's inside the wall during the consultation so there are no surprises.

Can I keep part of the wall?

Yes. Many homeowners keep a short wall, a half wall or a pair of columns to define the space, or turn part of the wall into a kitchen island or peninsula. It can also save money when part of the wall hides a pipe or duct.

Will the beam show after the wall comes out?

It depends on the joists and the span. A dropped beam sits below the ceiling and can be wrapped in drywall or wood. A flush beam is set up into the ceiling so the ceiling stays flat. Eric will tell you which your house allows and what each costs.

Is removing a wall messy?

There is dust, but it can be controlled. EBD hangs plastic barriers, protects floors and cleans as it goes. The loud, dusty part usually lasts only a day or two.

Do I need an engineer to remove a load-bearing wall?

Some openings can be sized from standard beam tables; longer spans, second-floor loads or point loads may need an engineered beam. EBD can coordinate the engineering when it's needed, and the inspector checks the work either way.

Can you open up a ceiling too?

Yes. EBD also removes flat ceilings to create vaulted or cathedral ceilings, often with exposed beams, which makes an opened-up room feel even larger.
