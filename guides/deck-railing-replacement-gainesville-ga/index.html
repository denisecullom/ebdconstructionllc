How Much Does Deck Railing Replacement Cost in Gainesville, GA?

Prices per foot for wood, composite, aluminum, cable and glass railings, what Georgia code requires, and whether your deck can keep its frame, for homeowners in Gainesville, Hall County and around Lake Lanier.

By Eric Bubniak, owner of EBD Construction LLC · November 16, 2026

Quick answer: New deck railing in Gainesville typically costs about $30 to $70 per linear foot for pressure-treated wood, $60 to $120 for composite, $70 to $140 for black aluminum or metal balusters, $100 to $200 for cable railing, and $150 to $300 for glass, installed. Most decks have 40 to 60 feet of railing, so expect about $1,500 to $4,000 in wood and $5,000 to $12,000 in cable. Which railing is best for a Lake Lanier or view lot?

Cable railing is the favorite on lake and mountain-view lots because thin stainless cables almost disappear when you sit down. Glass blocks even less of the view but costs more and needs cleaning. Black aluminum with thin balusters is a strong middle ground: clean-looking, nearly maintenance-free and less expensive than cable. Wood is the budget choice and fits a rustic cedar deck. What does Georgia code require for deck railings?

  • When a guard is required: any deck, porch or stair landing more than 30 inches above the ground.
  • Height: at least 36 inches from the deck surface to the top of the rail.
  • Openings: a 4-inch ball can't pass through anywhere, including between cables when they're pushed.
  • Strength: the top rail and posts have to hold a 200-pound load, which is why posts need proper blocking or hardware, not just a couple of bolts through a notched post.
  • Stairs: a graspable handrail 34 to 38 inches high on stairs with four or more risers.

Older Gainesville decks often fail on the last two points. A railing that wiggles when you lean on it is a safety problem, not just a cosmetic one.

Can new railing go on my old deck?

Usually, if the frame is sound. Eric checks the ledger, joists, beams and every railing post before pricing new rails. Rotted post bases, posts notched to half their thickness, or rusted hardware get replaced or reinforced. If the boards are worn too, it's often smart to resurface and re-rail at the same time. If the frame is failing, see our guide to whether you should repair or replace your deck.

How long does it take?

Wood, composite and aluminum railings usually take 1 to 3 days. Cable railing takes 2 to 5 days, because each post is drilled and each cable is tensioned. Cable, aluminum and glass systems can take 1 to 3 weeks to arrive, so we order as soon as you approve the design. Fall and early spring are great times to do it. Deck railing FAQs

How tall does deck railing have to be in Georgia?

Under the residential code used in Georgia, a deck more than 30 inches above the ground needs a guard at least 36 inches tall, with openings small enough that a 4-inch ball can't pass through. Stair handrails are usually 34 to 38 inches high, measured from the nose of the treads. Posts and rails also have to resist a 200-pound load at the top.

Is cable railing legal in Hall County?

Cable railing is allowed when it meets the same rules as any guard: at least 36 inches tall, strong enough, and tight enough that a 4-inch sphere can't push through the cables. That means stiff posts no more than about 4 to 5 feet apart, cables every 3 inches or so, and proper tensioning. We build cable railing to pass inspection.

Can I replace just the railing and keep my deck?

Often, yes. If the posts, joists, beams and the ledger attached to the house are solid, new railing can go on the existing frame. If the railing posts are rotted at the base or were notched and bolted weakly, we replace or reinforce the posts first. If the frame itself is failing, see our guide to repairing or replacing a deck.

How long does deck railing replacement take?

Most wood, composite and aluminum railings take 1 to 3 days on site. Cable railing usually takes 2 to 5 days because posts are drilled and cables are tensioned one by one. Ordering cable, aluminum or glass systems can add 1 to 3 weeks before the work starts.

Which deck railing lasts the longest?

Aluminum, stainless steel cable and glass need the least upkeep and can last 25 years or more. Composite railing lasts about 20 to 25 years with only washing. Pressure-treated wood lasts about 10 to 15 years in our humid Georgia climate and needs stain or paint every 2 to 3 years.

Do I need a permit to replace deck railing?

Replacing railing on an existing deck with the same type is often treated as a repair, but rebuilding posts, changing the stairs or building a new deck usually needs a permit in Gainesville and Hall County. Eric Bubniak checks and pulls any permit himself.
