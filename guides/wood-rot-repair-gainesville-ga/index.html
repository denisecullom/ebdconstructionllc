How do I know if I have rot?

  • The screwdriver test. Push a screwdriver into the wood. If it sinks in or the wood crumbles, it's rotting.
  • Paint that keeps peeling in the same spot, especially at the bottom of trim and door frames.
  • Dark stains, swelling or gaps where trim meets siding or the roof.
  • Soft or bouncy floors near a toilet, tub, dishwasher or exterior door.
  • Fungus, mushrooms or a musty smell in a crawl space or behind siding.
  • Carpenter bee or woodpecker holes. Woodpeckers often go after wood that's already soft or has insects in it.

Where does rot show up on Gainesville homes?

  • Fascia and rafter tails behind clogged or overflowing gutters.
  • Where a roof meets a wall without kick-out flashing, a very common spot on 1980s to 2000s homes.
  • Window sills and the bottom of door frames, where water sits on flat surfaces.
  • Porch columns, floors and steps, especially on shady lots near Lake Lanier that stay damp.
  • Deck ledger boards and joists without flashing. See our deck repair or replace guide.
  • Sill plates and rim joists in damp crawl spaces or where soil is piled against the siding.

Repair or replace?

A shallow soft spot on trim can be dug out and rebuilt with two-part wood epoxy, then primed and painted. Anything soft through the board, structural, or bigger than a few inches should be cut out and replaced. For exterior trim we often replace rotten wood with PVC or composite trim, which can't rot, or with primed, treated or fiber cement material. When rot is widespread across the house, new siding can be the better investment; see our siding and exterior remodeling guide.

How do I keep rot from coming back?

  • Fix the water first. Clean or add gutters and downspout extensions, and add kick-out flashing where roofs meet walls.
  • Keep wood off the ground and roof. Siding and trim should sit 6 to 8 inches above soil and 1 to 2 inches above a roof.
  • Caulk and paint. Repaint exterior wood about every 7 to 10 years and re-caulk joints as they open up.
  • Aim sprinklers away from the house and keep shrubs trimmed back so walls can dry.
  • Use rot-proof materials on the spots that keep failing, like PVC trim at door frames and window sills.
  • Keep the crawl space dry with a vapor barrier and working vents or encapsulation.

Is it rot or termites?

Rot is soft and spongy, often darker, and cracks across the grain. Termite damage has tunnels that follow the grain, often packed with dirt, and termites leave mud tubes on the foundation. If you see mud tubes, call a pest control company first, then we can repair the damaged wood.

Does insurance pay for rot?

Usually not. Most homeowners policies exclude rot from slow, long-term moisture or lack of maintenance. Rot caused by a sudden covered event, like a burst pipe or a storm that opens the roof, may be covered. Check your policy or ask your agent. For damage from a leak, see our roof replacement guide.

Wood rot repair FAQs

How do I know if wood is rotten?

Press a screwdriver into the wood. If it sinks in easily, feels spongy or the wood crumbles, it's rotting. Other signs are paint that keeps peeling in the same spot, dark stains, gaps where trim meets siding, mushrooms or fungus, and carpenter bee or woodpecker holes.

Can rotten wood be repaired or does it have to be replaced?

Small, shallow spots on trim can be dug out and rebuilt with wood epoxy for about $150 to $600. Anything that's soft through the board, structural, or larger than a few inches should be cut out and replaced with new wood or PVC trim, and the water source fixed.

What causes wood rot on houses in Georgia?

Water that keeps getting in and can't dry out. In North Georgia the usual causes are clogged or missing gutters, missing kick-out flashing where a roof meets a wall, siding or trim too close to the ground or roof, failed caulk and paint, sprinklers hitting the house, and leaks at windows, doors and toilets.

Is wood rot covered by homeowners insurance?

Usually not. Most policies exclude rot from slow, long-term moisture or lack of maintenance. Rot caused by a sudden covered event, like a burst pipe, may be covered. Check your policy or ask your agent.

Is it rot or termites?

Rot is soft and spongy and often darker, with cracks across the grain. Termite damage has tunnels or galleries that follow the grain, often packed with dirt, and mud tubes on the foundation. If you see mud tubes, call a pest control company before the repair.

How long does wood rot repair take?

Most trim, fascia and door repairs take one to three days, including primer and paint. Structural repairs to a sill plate, rim joist or porch framing usually take three days to two weeks.
