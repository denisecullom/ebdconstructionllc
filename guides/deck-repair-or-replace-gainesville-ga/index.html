What are the warning signs a deck is unsafe?

Check these, especially on a deck more than 15 years old:

  • The ledger board. This board bolts the deck to the house and is where most deck collapses start. Look for rot, missing bolts (nails alone aren't enough), and no metal flashing above it.
  • Soft or spongy wood. Push a screwdriver into joists, posts and boards. If it sinks in easily, that wood is rotting.
  • Wobbly railings. Push hard on the rail. It shouldn't move. Rails should be at least 36 inches tall, with gaps between balusters too small for a 4-inch ball to pass through.
  • Posts and footings. Posts buried directly in the dirt, leaning posts, or sinking concrete footings.
  • Rusted fasteners and hangers. Older galvanized nails and joist hangers corrode, especially against treated lumber.
  • Loose or uneven stairs. Stairs fail before the deck does. Treads should be solid, evenly spaced, with a handrail you can grip.
  • Bounce. A deck that bounces or sways when people walk across it may be undersized or missing bracing.

How long does a deck last in Georgia?

A pressure-treated pine deck typically lasts about 15 to 25 years in North Georgia, depending on how well it's sealed and how much sun and shade it gets. Shady decks near Lake Lanier stay damp longer and rot sooner. Cedar lasts about as long with regular sealing. Composite decking can last 25 to 30 years or more, but the treated wood frame underneath still ages, so it should be checked too. Cleaning and sealing a wood deck every 2 to 3 years adds years to its life.

How much does deck repair cost in Gainesville, GA?

Small repairs, like replacing a few boards, tightening or rebuilding a rail section, or resetting a stair, usually run $500 to $2,500. Bigger repairs, like a new ledger with flashing, new posts and footings, or a full set of stairs, usually run $2,000 to $6,000. Do I need a permit to repair or replace a deck in Hall County?

Replacing a few boards or a rail section usually doesn't need a permit. Rebuilding the structure, replacing stairs or footings, or building a new deck does, with footing, framing and final inspections. Homes in Gainesville city limits are permitted through the City of Gainesville and homes in unincorporated Hall County through Hall County; Flowery Branch, Oakwood and Buford handle their own. Lake Lanier docks and shoreline work are handled separately by the Army Corps of Engineers. EBD Construction pulls all required permits itself.

Deck repair and replacement FAQs

Can you put new boards on an old deck frame?

Yes, if the frame is sound. Resurfacing saves the cost of a new frame and footings. We check every joist and the ledger first, and replace any bad pieces before the new boards go on.

Can you replace just the deck stairs?

Yes. Stairs often wear out before the deck does. New stairs with solid stringers, a landing on long runs and a sturdy handrail typically cost $1,000 to $5,000.

Is it worth replacing a wood deck with composite?

If you plan to stay, often yes. Composite costs more up front but doesn't need sanding or sealing, and it won't splinter. Pressure-treated pine is still the lowest-cost option.

How often should a deck be inspected?

Look it over yourself every spring, and have a contractor check the ledger, posts and stairs every few years once the deck is 10 to 15 years old.

Yes. Lake decks are often tall and on steep lots, so they need careful inspection. See the Lake Lanier guide for more lake-home projects.
