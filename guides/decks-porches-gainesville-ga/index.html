How much does a deck or porch cost in Gainesville, GA?

Cost depends mostly on size, height off the ground, materials, and whether it has a roof. A roof is the biggest jump in price, because it adds footings, posts, beams, rafters, roofing, gutters and usually electrical. Steep lots, which are common around Lake Lanier, add cost for taller posts and more stairs.

Pressure-treated, cedar or composite: which is best in Georgia?

  • Pressure-treated pine is the most affordable and resists rot and termites. It needs sealing or staining every couple of years to keep it from checking and graying in Georgia sun. The framing under almost every deck, whatever the surface, is pressure-treated.
  • Cedar is naturally rot- and insect-resistant, stays more stable than pine, and takes stain beautifully. It costs more than pine and still needs regular sealing.
  • Composite costs the most up front but doesn't need staining, won't splinter, and holds its color. Darker colors can get hot in full summer sun, so lighter shades or a roof help.

For railings, cable railing is popular on lake and view lots because it barely blocks the view. Metal balusters are a durable, clean-looking middle ground, and wood railings are the most budget-friendly.

How long does it take to build a deck or porch?

Once permits are approved and materials are on site, most open decks take about 1 to 3 weeks to build. Covered and screened porches usually take about 4 to 10 weeks, because the roof, electrical, ceiling and screens each add steps and inspections. Permit review typically adds a few weeks before work starts, and rain days can stretch any outdoor schedule.

Do I need a permit to build a deck or porch in Gainesville or Hall County?

Yes. Hall County lists decks, porches and other additions among the projects that require a building permit, and the City of Gainesville, Flowery Branch, Oakwood and Buford each require permits for decks and porches within their limits too. Expect inspections of the footings, the framing and the finished structure. Under the residential building code Georgia uses, a deck more than 30 inches above the ground needs a guardrail, and stairs must meet rules for rise, run and handrails. EBD Construction pulls all required permits itself and schedules every inspection, so you don't have to.

Deck and porch FAQs

What is the best time of year to build a deck in Georgia?

Decks and porches can be built most of the year in Gainesville. Late winter and early spring are popular so the space is ready for summer, and fall is a good time because the weather is dry and mild. Booking a few months ahead gives you the most choice of start dates, since spring fills up fast.

Can you build a roof over my existing deck?

Sometimes. A roof adds a lot of weight, so the existing footings, posts and beams have to be strong enough to carry it. Many older decks weren't built for that, and it's often better to rebuild the frame. Eric checks this during the free consultation.

Is a screened porch worth it in Gainesville?

For many families, yes. Mosquitoes, gnats and spring pollen make open decks hard to enjoy for much of the year in North Georgia. A screened porch with a ceiling fan lets you use the space from early spring through late fall.

How long does a deck last?

A well-built pressure-treated or cedar deck commonly lasts 15 to 20 years or more with regular cleaning and sealing, and composite decking often lasts longer. The framing underneath and how it's attached to the house matter as much as the surface boards.

Should I repair or replace my old deck?

If the frame, posts and attachment to the house are sound, replacing worn boards and railings can be enough. Soft or rotted framing, wobbly railings, rusted connectors or a deck that pulls away from the house are signs it should be rebuilt. EBD can take a look and tell you which makes sense.
