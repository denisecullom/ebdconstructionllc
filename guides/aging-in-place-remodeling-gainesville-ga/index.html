What are the most important aging-in-place upgrades?

Most falls at home happen in the bathroom and on stairs, so start there. In order of impact:

  1. A walk-in or curbless shower with a built-in bench and a handheld sprayer.
  2. Grab bars at the toilet, in the shower and by the tub, anchored to framing, not drywall anchors.
  3. Handrails on both sides of every staircase, inside and out, including deck and porch steps.
  4. Better lighting in halls, stairs and bathrooms, with switches at both ends and night lighting.
  5. Slip-resistant flooring such as textured tile or luxury vinyl plank, and no loose rugs or thresholds.
  6. Wider doors and lever handles so a walker or wheelchair fits and doors open with a closed fist.
  7. A zero-step entry from the garage, driveway or back deck.
  8. A bedroom and full bath on the main floor, so stairs become optional.

How wide should doorways be for a wheelchair or walker?

Plan for a 36-inch door, which gives about 32 inches of clear opening, the width accessibility guidelines use for a wheelchair. Many older Gainesville homes have 28- to 30-inch bedroom and bathroom doors. Widening one means a new header, frame and trim. If the wall is load-bearing, Eric sizes the header and pulls a permit. Where there isn't room to swing a door, a pocket door or sliding barn door opens the full width without blocking the hall. A wheelchair also needs about a 5-foot circle to turn around in a bathroom.

Should I add a first-floor bedroom or move?

If the house is paid for, close to family and the main floor works except for the bedroom, adding a main-floor suite usually costs less than selling, paying Realtor commissions and moving. Options include converting a den or dining room (often the least expensive), finishing a walk-out basement with its own entrance, converting the garage, or building an addition. Do I need a permit for aging-in-place remodeling in Hall County?

It depends on the work. Grab bars, lever handles and light fixtures usually don't need one. Moving plumbing for a new shower, widening a doorway in a load-bearing wall, building a ramp or deck, and adding a room do. Homes in Gainesville city limits are permitted through the City of Gainesville and homes in unincorporated Hall County through Hall County. EBD Construction pulls all required permits itself.

Does insurance or Medicare pay for home modifications?

Usually not. Original Medicare generally does not pay for grab bars, showers or ramps, though some Medicare Advantage plans offer limited safety benefits. Other places to look:

  • Taxes. The IRS lets you count changes made mainly for medical care (ramps, widened doors, grab bars, lowered cabinets) as a medical expense if you itemize. Ask your tax preparer.
  • Veterans. The VA offers home modification grants, such as HISA and SAH grants, for eligible veterans.
  • Local help. The Legacy Link Area Agency on Aging serves Hall County and the surrounding North Georgia counties and can point you to programs.

Aging-in-place remodeling FAQs

What is a curbless shower?

A shower with no step or curb to climb over. The floor slopes gently to the drain so a wheelchair or walker can roll straight in. It usually requires lowering the subfloor, which a contractor plans during the remodel.

Will grab bars make my bathroom look like a hospital?

No. Grab bars come in brushed nickel, matte black and brass finishes to match your fixtures, and some double as towel bars or toilet paper holders.

What height should a toilet be for seniors?

A comfort-height or "right height" toilet sits about 17 to 19 inches high, a few inches taller than a standard toilet, which makes sitting and standing easier.

Can you add a stair railing to both sides?

Yes. A second handrail is one of the least expensive and most effective safety upgrades. EBD builds rails for interior stairs, porches and decks.

Do you build wheelchair ramps?

Yes. EBD builds wood ramps with handrails and landings, and where the grade allows, a gently sloped walkway to a zero-step door. A common ramp slope is 1 inch of rise per foot of length.
