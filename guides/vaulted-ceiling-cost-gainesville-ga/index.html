How Much Does It Cost to Vault a Ceiling in Gainesville, GA?

Prices for turning a flat ceiling into a vaulted or cathedral ceiling, why trusses change the cost, and cheaper ways to get height and beams, for homeowners in Gainesville, Hall County and around Lake Lanier.

By Eric Bubniak, owner of EBD Construction LLC · November 20, 2026

Quick answer: Vaulting a flat ceiling in one room in Gainesville typically costs about $15,000 to $45,000 under a rafter-framed roof and $25,000 to $75,000+ when trusses have to be replaced or re-engineered. If you want the look for less, box beams on an existing vault run about $2,000 to $10,000 and a tray or coffered ceiling $3,000 to $15,000. Rafters or trusses: why does it matter?

Look in the attic. Rafters are single sloped boards running from the wall up to a ridge board, with separate ceiling joists across the bottom. Those ceiling joists keep the walls from spreading, so we replace their job with a structural ridge beam or high collar ties before removing them. That's the less expensive kind of vault and common in older Gainesville homes.

Trusses are factory-built triangles with diagonal webs, usually 2 feet apart, found in most homes built since the 1970s. Every piece of a truss carries load, so you can't just cut out the bottom. They're replaced with scissor trusses, which have a sloped bottom, or reinforced to an engineer's design. That means opening part of the roof, so it costs more and is best planned with a roof replacement if one is due.

What else has to be planned?

  • Attic mechanicals. HVAC ducts, bath fan and plumbing vents, and wiring get rerouted.
  • Insulation. The new ceiling is right under the roof, so it needs proper insulation and ventilation to stay comfortable and dry in Georgia summers.
  • Lighting and fans. Sloped-ceiling recessed cans, pendants, a fan on an angled mount, and switches for each.
  • Walls and windows. Taller gable walls can take transom or clerestory windows for more light.
  • Finish. Smooth drywall, tongue-and-groove pine or shiplap, and stained or painted beams.

Are there cheaper ways to get the look?

Yes. A tray ceiling steps up 8 to 12 inches in the middle of a room and needs no roof work. A coffered ceiling adds a grid of beams to a flat ceiling. Box beams or tongue-and-groove wood dress up an existing vault or a flat ceiling. Taking a ceiling up into an unused attic over a bonus room or building an addition with a vaulted ceiling from the start are other options.

Vaulted ceiling FAQs

Can any ceiling be vaulted?

Most can, but the roof framing decides the cost. A roof built with rafters and ceiling joists, common in older homes, can usually be vaulted by adding a structural ridge beam or collar ties. A roof built with factory trusses, common in homes since the 1970s, can't simply have the bottom chords cut out; the trusses have to be replaced with scissor trusses or reinforced to an engineer's design. Ductwork, plumbing vents and wiring in the attic also have to be moved.

Do I need a structural engineer to vault a ceiling?

Almost always. Removing ceiling joists or truss chords changes how the roof holds itself together, so a structural engineer specifies the ridge beam, ties or new trusses. Engineering typically costs about $500 to $2,000, and the permit office in Gainesville or Hall County will want the plans.

How long does it take to vault a ceiling?

Engineering and permits usually take 2 to 4 weeks. The construction itself typically takes 2 to 4 weeks for a rafter roof and 3 to 6 weeks when trusses are replaced, including insulation, drywall, paint, lighting and any beams.

Does a vaulted ceiling raise energy bills?

A taller room has more air to heat and cool, and the insulation space in a vaulted ceiling is thinner than an open attic. Insulating the new roof line properly, often with closed-cell spray foam or rigid foam plus batts, keeps the room comfortable. Insulation typically adds about $3,000 to $8,000, and a ceiling fan helps push warm air down in winter.

Are faux beams cheaper than a real vaulted ceiling?

Yes. Hollow box beams made of wood or reclaimed lumber can go on an existing vaulted ceiling, or even a flat one, for about $100 to $300 per linear foot installed, typically $2,000 to $10,000 for a room. They give the look of exposed timber without structural work.

Do I need a permit to vault a ceiling?

Yes. Removing ceiling joists or changing roof trusses is structural work and needs a building permit and inspections in Gainesville and Hall County. Eric Bubniak pulls all permits himself.
