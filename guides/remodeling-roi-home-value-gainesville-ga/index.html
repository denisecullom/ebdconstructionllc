Which Home Remodels Add the Most Value in Gainesville, GA?

What each project costs, how much of it comes back when you sell, and how to spend wisely on a home in Gainesville, Flowery Branch, Oakwood, Lake Lanier and across Hall County.

By Eric Bubniak, owner of EBD Construction LLC · October 15, 2026

Quick answer: The remodels that return the biggest share of their cost at resale are usually small, visible exterior updates and modest refreshes: a new entry or garage door, stone veneer, fresh siding, a deck, and a minor kitchen remodel. National Cost vs. Value data shows these often recoup about 80% to more than 100% of what they cost. Big projects like a major kitchen overhaul, an upscale bathroom or a primary suite addition usually recoup about 20% to 50% on paper, but they add the space and function buyers in Gainesville pay for and that you get to enjoy for years. In Hall County, screened porches, a primary bedroom on the main level, finished basements and updated kitchens are the upgrades buyers ask about most. Which remodeling projects have the best return on investment?

The pattern is the same every year: the smaller and more visible the project, the bigger the share of its cost that comes back at resale. Large projects add more dollars of value, but they also cost a lot more, so the percentage is lower.

What upgrades do buyers in Gainesville and Hall County want most?

National averages are a starting point. Around Gainesville, the projects that make a house stand out to local buyers are:

  • Screened and covered porches. Mosquitoes, gnats and spring pollen make open decks hard to enjoy for much of the year. A screened porch is one of the first things Hall County buyers look for. See our screened porch vs. sunroom guide.
  • A primary bedroom on the main level. Many buyers moving to the Lake Lanier area are retiring or planning ahead, and a main-floor primary suite widens your pool of buyers. Our aging-in-place guide covers first-floor suites and walk-in showers.
  • An updated kitchen with an open layout. Closed-off kitchens in 1980s and 1990s homes are a common reason houses sit. Removing a wall often makes the biggest difference for the money.
  • Finished basements. Hall County's hilly lots mean lots of walk-out basements, and finishing one adds living space for far less than an addition. See our basement finishing guide.
  • An extra bedroom or bathroom. Going from three bedrooms to four, or two baths to two and a half, moves a house into a higher price bracket in many neighborhoods.
  • Outdoor living on Lake Lanier. On lake homes, decks and porches facing the water carry more weight than almost anything inside. See our Lake Lanier guide.

Which remodels don't add much value?

Some projects are worth doing for your own enjoyment, but don't expect them to pay you back when you sell:

  • Over-improving for the street. If your house ends up worth far more than the others around it, buyers won't pay the full difference. A $90,000 kitchen in a neighborhood of $300,000 homes rarely comes back.
  • Very personal design choices. Bold tile, unusual layouts and niche rooms can narrow your pool of buyers.
  • Converting the only garage in a neighborhood where buyers expect one. A garage conversion can be great for your family but can hurt resale in some subdivisions.
  • Removing the last bathtub. Swapping the primary tub for a big shower is popular, but keep at least one tub in the house for families with young kids.
  • Unpermitted work. Space that isn't on record may not count toward square footage and can stall a sale. Our permits guide explains why it matters.

How much should I spend on a remodel compared to my home's value?

A common rule of thumb is to keep any single room under about 10% to 15% of your home's value, and a kitchen in the range of about 5% to 15%. On a $400,000 home in Gainesville, that means a kitchen budget of roughly $20,000 to $60,000 keeps you in line with the market. If you plan to stay ten years or more, it's reasonable to spend more on the rooms you use every day, because you're buying the experience as much as the resale value.

Should I remodel before selling my house?

Usually, do the small things and skip the big ones. Before listing, the best return comes from:

  1. Fixing what an inspector will flag: soft deck boards, roof leaks, rotten trim, loose railings and drainage problems.
  2. Fresh paint inside and out in neutral colors.
  3. Flooring where carpet is worn or rooms don't match. See what new flooring costs.
  4. A light kitchen and bath refresh: painted cabinets, new hardware, lighting and faucets. See paint, reface or replace your cabinets.
  5. Curb appeal: a new front door, trim and siding repairs, and a clean, solid front porch (see what a new front porch costs).

A pre-listing refresh like this typically runs about $3,000 to $20,000 around Gainesville and can be done in one to four weeks. A full kitchen or bathroom remodel right before selling rarely pays back, because you're paying for it and the buyer gets to enjoy it.

How long do I need to stay for a remodel to pay off?

If you plan to stay five years or more, choose projects mostly for how you live, and let resale value be the tiebreaker. Within two or three years of selling, stick to repairs, paint and the high-return exterior and kitchen updates above. Remodeling value FAQs

Does adding a bedroom or bathroom increase home value?

Usually, yes. Adding a fourth bedroom or a half bath can move a house into a higher price bracket in many Gainesville neighborhoods. Additions typically recoup about 20% to 40% of their cost on paper, but the added space often matters more to buyers than the percentage suggests.

Does a screened porch add value in Georgia?

Yes. With mosquitoes, gnats and spring pollen, a screened porch is one of the first features Hall County buyers look for. Decks recoup about 85% to 95% of their cost nationally, and a well-built screened porch is a strong selling point on top of that.

Does a finished basement add value?

Yes. Finishing a basement adds living space for far less per square foot than an addition, and walk-out basements are common on Hall County's sloped lots. Permitted finished space also counts when the house is appraised and sold.

Is a new roof worth it before selling?

A new roof recoups only about 50% to 60% of its cost on average, but an old or leaking roof can scare off buyers or lead to a big credit at closing. If the roof is near the end of its life, repairing or replacing it before listing usually helps the sale go smoothly.

Do I need permits for remodeling work to count when I sell?

Permitted work is on record and counts when the house is appraised and sold. Unpermitted additions, basements and garage conversions may not count toward square footage and can stall a sale. EBD Construction pulls every permit itself.
