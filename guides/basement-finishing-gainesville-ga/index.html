Do I need a permit to finish a basement in Hall County?

Yes. Turning an unfinished basement into living space needs a building permit, with inspections for framing, electrical, plumbing and insulation before the drywall goes up. Homes in Gainesville city limits are permitted through the City of Gainesville and homes in unincorporated Hall County through Hall County; Flowery Branch, Oakwood and Buford handle their own. A permitted basement also counts when you sell, because the finished space can be listed and appraised. EBD Construction pulls all required permits itself.

Does a basement bedroom need an egress window?

Yes. Under the building code Georgia uses, every basement bedroom needs an emergency escape opening, either a large enough window or a door to the outside. On a walk-out basement, the exterior door or a full-size window on the low side of the house usually solves it. In a basement that's fully below ground, a new egress window with a window well may be needed. Habitable rooms also need enough ceiling height, generally about 7 feet. Eric checks both at the first visit.

How do you keep a Georgia basement dry?

Georgia's red clay holds water, so moisture is the first thing to check. Before finishing, EBD looks for signs of water: stains, efflorescence (white powder on the block), musty smells and wet corners after rain. Common fixes include regrading and extending downspouts outside, sealing cracks, adding a drain or sump where needed, and using moisture-resistant materials like rigid foam insulation, mold-resistant drywall and waterproof vinyl plank flooring. A dehumidifier or the HVAC system keeps humidity down in summer.

Can you add a bathroom to a basement?

Yes. If your basement has plumbing rough-ins, adding a bathroom is straightforward. If the sewer line runs higher than the basement floor, an ejector pump or an up-flush system lifts the waste to the main line. A half bath typically starts around $6,000; a full bath with a tile shower costs more. See the bathroom remodeling guide for fixture and tile options.

How long does it take to finish a basement?

Most basements take about 4 to 10 weeks of work once permits are issued, depending on size and whether a bathroom or kitchenette is included. A typical EBD basement goes like this:

  1. Free consultation. Eric looks at the space, moisture, ceiling height and egress, and talks through how you'll use it.
  2. Layout, estimate and permit. You get a written estimate, pick finishes, and Eric pulls the permit.
  3. Framing and rough-ins. Walls, plumbing, electrical and HVAC go in, then get inspected.
  4. Insulation and drywall. Followed by an insulation inspection where required.
  5. Finishes. Flooring, doors, trim, paint, tile and fixtures.
  6. Final inspection and walkthrough.

Is finishing a basement worth it?

Usually, yes. A finished basement is one of the least expensive ways to add living space because the foundation, roof and walls already exist. It typically costs far less per square foot than a room addition, and a walk-out basement with its own entrance makes a great guest suite or in-law apartment. If you need more space but don't have a basement, see whether to remodel, add on or build new.

Basement finishing FAQs

Can I live at home while my basement is finished?

Yes. Basement work is separate from the main living space, so most families live at home normally. EBD keeps dust contained and the work area closed off at the end of each day.

What is the best flooring for a basement in Georgia?

Waterproof luxury vinyl plank and tile are the most common choices because they handle moisture. Carpet can work in bedrooms if the space stays dry.

Can I add a kitchenette or wet bar in the basement?

Yes. A wet bar or kitchenette is popular for guest suites and game rooms. Costs depend on plumbing, cabinets and appliances.

Does a finished basement count as square footage?

A permitted, finished basement can be listed as finished living space when you sell, though it is often reported separately from above-grade square footage.
