What kinds of home additions are there?

Most additions in the Gainesville area fall into one of these types:

  1. Bump-out. A small extension, usually under 100 square feet, that pushes one wall out a few feet to enlarge a kitchen, bathroom or closet.
  2. Single room addition. A new bedroom, home office, den or playroom built on the side or back of the house.
  3. Family room or great room addition. A larger living space, often with a vaulted ceiling and big windows facing the backyard or the lake.
  4. Primary suite addition. A bedroom with its own bathroom and walk-in closet, often on the main floor so you can age in place.
  5. Sunroom. A bright room with walls of windows, built as a true heated and cooled room or as a three-season space.
  6. Second-story addition. Building up over part or all of the house when the lot has no room to build out.
  7. Garage or in-law suite. A new attached garage, or a converted or added space for a parent or adult child to live in.

If you aren't sure which one fits, that's what the free consultation is for. Eric will look at your house, your lot and how you want to use the space, and tell you what makes sense.

How much does a home addition cost in Gainesville, GA?

The cost of a home addition in Gainesville depends mostly on five things: the size of the addition, the foundation, how the roof ties in, whether it includes a bathroom or kitchen, and the finishes you choose. As a rule of thumb, many ground-level additions in the area land around $75 to $300 per square foot.

Is it cheaper to build an addition or move?

For a lot of Gainesville and Hall County homeowners, yes. Moving means agent commissions, closing costs, moving expenses and buying a bigger house at today's prices and interest rates. An addition lets you keep your lot, your neighbors, your schools and, if you're near Lake Lanier, your view. It also adds square footage that counts toward your home's value. A free consultation gives you a real addition price so you can compare the two side by side.

Is it cheaper to build up or out?

Building out is usually cheaper. A ground-level addition gets its own new foundation, and the rest of the house stays mostly untouched. Building up means checking and often reinforcing the existing foundation and walls, removing the roof, and adding a staircase, which takes up space on the floor below. A second story makes sense when the lot is small, setbacks leave no room to build outward, or you'd lose too much of the yard.

How long does a room addition take?

Most home additions in the Gainesville area take about 3 to 5 months for a single room addition and 4 to 8 months for a primary suite or second story, counting design and permitting. A small bump-out can often be done in 4 to 8 weeks. Construction itself is usually the shorter part; design, engineering and permits take time up front, and weather can affect foundation and roofing days.

Do I need a permit for a home addition in Gainesville or Hall County?

Yes. Every room addition needs a building permit, with inspections at the foundation, framing, electrical, plumbing, mechanical and final stages. Homes inside Gainesville city limits are permitted through the City of Gainesville; homes in unincorporated Hall County go through Hall County. Flowery Branch, Oakwood and Buford each have their own process. During permitting, the plans are checked against setbacks (how close you can build to the property line) and other zoning rules. If your home is on septic and the addition adds a bedroom, the septic system usually has to be reviewed for capacity. If you live in a neighborhood with an HOA, plan on getting their approval too. EBD Construction pulls all required permits itself as part of the project, so you don't have to.

Can you match my existing house?

Yes, and it's one of the most important parts of a good addition. EBD matches the roof pitch, shingles, siding, trim, windows and floor height so the new space looks like it was always there. When an exact match isn't available, such as with older siding, the crew can re-side a full wall or the whole house so everything is consistent. EBD also handles siding, roofing and exterior paint, so the outside of the house can be updated in the same project.

Home addition FAQs

Can I live in my house during an addition?

Usually, yes. Most of an addition is built outside your existing walls, and the opening into the house is cut near the end of the job. You'll have noise and crew on site during work hours, but most families stay home the whole time.

Does a home addition add value?

Added square footage, especially a bedroom or a primary suite with its own bathroom, generally raises a home's value and makes it appeal to more buyers. How much depends on your neighborhood and how well the addition matches the house, which is why matching the roofline and exterior matters.

Do I need an architect for an addition?

Not always. EBD designs additions and can coordinate any drawings or engineering the permit requires, such as for a second story or a large beam. If you already have an architect, EBD can build from their plans.

What foundation will my addition need?

Most additions match the existing house: a crawl space if your home is on a crawl space, a slab if it's on a slab, and sometimes a basement on sloped Hall County lots. Matching the foundation keeps the floors level between the old and new rooms.

Will my heating and air handle the new space?

Sometimes. A small room may tie into your current system, while a larger addition or primary suite often needs a new or upsized unit or a ductless mini-split. This is checked during planning and included in the estimate.
