What are the signs of a sagging floor?

  • A dip or slope you can feel walking across a room, or a marble that rolls to one spot.
  • Bouncy or springy floors, or dishes that rattle when someone walks by.
  • Doors that stick or swing open on their own, and cracks in drywall above doorways.
  • Gaps under baseboards, cracked floor tile or grout, and squeaks that keep getting worse.
  • A soft or spongy spot near a tub, toilet, sink or exterior door, which usually means rot.

What causes floors to sag around Gainesville?

Most homes in Gainesville and Hall County built before the 1990s sit on a crawl space, and that's where the trouble starts. Common causes are joists that were undersized or spaced too far apart, support beams with piers too far apart, joists cut or notched for plumbing and ductwork, rot from crawl space humidity or a long-term leak, termite damage, and piers that settled in red clay. Older ranch and split-level homes are especially prone. See our guide to remodeling an older ranch home.

How is a sagging floor fixed?

  1. Find the cause. Eric goes into the crawl space to check joists, beams, piers, sills and moisture, and takes level readings across the floor above.
  2. Fix the cause. Leaks, standing water, poor drainage or termites get handled first, or the repair won't last.
  3. Replace or reinforce. Rotted wood is cut out, weak joists are sistered with new lumber, and new beams and posts go on proper footings.
  4. Lift slowly. The floor is raised a little at a time, often over days or weeks, so drywall, tile and doors above aren't damaged. Some old sag is set in the lumber and is better reinforced in place than forced flat.
  5. Finish above. If the subfloor or finished floor is damaged, we replace it in the same project.

What about crawl space moisture?

Damp crawl spaces are the root of a lot of rot in North Georgia. Fixing gutters and grading so water drains away from the house is the first step. A vapor barrier on the ground is a modest cost, and full crawl space encapsulation by a specialist typically runs about $5,000 to $15,000. If the floor sagged because of water damage, see what water damage repair costs, and for rot at the sills and siding, see our wood rot repair guide.

When do I need a foundation company or engineer?

Most sagging floors are a framing problem, which a general contractor fixes. If the block or brick foundation wall is cracked, leaning or moving, footings have sunk badly, or the damage runs through much of the house, a structural engineer should design the repair and a foundation specialist may handle the foundation itself. We'll tell you honestly which kind of problem you have, and we coordinate with the engineer when one is needed.

Sagging floor FAQs

What causes floors to sag in Georgia homes?

The most common causes are joists that were undersized or spaced too far apart when the house was built, beams and piers spaced too far apart, joists cut or notched for plumbing and ductwork, rot from crawl space moisture or a long-term leak, termite damage, and piers or footings that settled in our red clay soil. Heavy loads like a tiled floor, a piano or a large aquarium can make an existing weakness show up.

Are sagging floors dangerous?

Most sagging floors are a slow problem, not an emergency, but they get worse over time and can crack tile and drywall, make doors stick and let plumbing pull apart. A floor that feels soft or spongy, a sudden new dip, or rot you can push a screwdriver into should be looked at soon.

Can a sagging floor be leveled from the crawl space?

Usually, yes. In most Gainesville crawl-space homes we work from below: adding a new beam and posts on proper footings, sistering new lumber to weak joists, replacing rotted wood, and jacking the floor up slowly. We lift a little at a time over days or weeks so the drywall, tile and doors above aren't damaged.

Do I need a structural engineer for a sagging floor?

Not always. Common joist and beam repairs can often be done to code with standard spans. If the foundation wall is cracked or moving, footings have sunk, the damage is widespread, or the inspector asks for one, a structural engineer designs the repair, typically for about $400 to $1,200, and we build to the plan.

How long does sagging floor repair take?

Most crawl space framing repairs take 1 to 5 days. Larger releveling jobs take 1 to 3 weeks, partly because the floor is raised gradually. Replacing finished flooring above the repair adds a few more days.
