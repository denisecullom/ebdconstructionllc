Remodeling or Building a Home on Lake Lanier

Decks, screened porches, walk-out basements and new lake homes on the Hall County side of Lake Lanier, plus what to know about the Corps of Engineers.

By Eric Bubniak, owner of EBD Construction LLC · October 8, 2026

Quick answer: Lake Lanier homes usually come down to three kinds of projects: decks and screened porches facing the water, walk-out basements, and kitchens and great rooms with a view. Typical ranges start around $2,000 for a deck and $5,000 for basement work, and new lake homes run about $150 to $350 or more per square foot. Work on your own lot is permitted through the county, while anything on Corps of Engineers shoreline needs a separate Corps permit. Do I need a Corps of Engineers permit to build on Lake Lanier?

It depends on where the work is. Lake Lanier is managed by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, and in many places the Corps owns a strip of land between the water and private lots. A "lakefront" lot often stops at that Corps line instead of the water's edge.

  • Work on your own lot (the house, decks, porches, basements, additions) is permitted through the county or city like any other home project. EBD pulls these permits itself.
  • Work on Corps property (docks, paths, steps, clearing trees or brush toward the water) needs a Shoreline Use Permit from the Corps' Lake Lanier office, and is handled separately from the house.

Before you plan a deck or addition close to the back of the lot, it's worth confirming where your property line and the Corps boundary are. Eric can look at your survey with you during the consultation.

What should I know about building on a sloped lake lot?

  • Tall decks need engineering. Decks one or two stories up need properly sized posts, footings, beams and lateral bracing. EBD builds them to code and gets them inspected.
  • Walk-out basements are an asset. Most sloped lake lots have one, and finishing it adds a family room or guest suite with its own door to the yard. See the basement finishing guide.
  • Drainage matters. Water running down the hill needs somewhere to go before it reaches the foundation or the lake.
  • Access can be tight. Steep driveways and narrow lots can affect how materials get delivered, which is worth planning before work starts.

Screened porch or open deck on a Lake Lanier house?

Most lake owners want both. An open deck is best for sun and the full view; a screened porch keeps out mosquitoes and spring pollen and gets used every evening from April to October. A common solution is a screened porch on the main level with an open deck or patio below, or an open deck with a screened section. See screened porch vs. sunroom and the deck and porch guide for more.

Lake Lanier home FAQs

Do you build docks on Lake Lanier?

EBD focuses on the house and everything on your lot: decks, porches, basements, kitchens, additions and new homes. Docks sit on Corps of Engineers property and are permitted separately through the Corps.

Can you replace an old deck on my lake house?

Yes. Replacing worn decks on lake homes is a common project, often with cedar or composite boards and cable railing to keep the view open.

Can you screen in my existing deck?

Often, yes, if the framing and footings can carry a roof. Eric will check the structure first. Screening in an existing deck or porch typically runs about $2,000 to $12,000.

How do I know where my property ends and the Corps land starts?

Your survey or plat shows the boundary, and the Corps line is often marked on the ground. Eric can review it with you before planning a deck or addition near the back of the lot.

Can you finish my walk-out basement?

Yes. Finishing a walk-out basement into a family room, bedroom or guest suite is one of the most popular lake-home projects.
