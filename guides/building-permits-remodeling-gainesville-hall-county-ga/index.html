Building Permits for Remodeling in Gainesville and Hall County, GA

Which projects need a permit, who issues it, what it costs and how long it takes in Gainesville, Flowery Branch, Oakwood, Buford, Murrayville, Lake Lanier and across Hall County.

By Eric Bubniak, owner of EBD Construction LLC · October 14, 2026

Quick answer: In Gainesville and Hall County you usually need a building permit when a project changes the structure, plumbing, electrical or HVAC, or adds living space. That covers most kitchen and bathroom remodels, wall removal, decks, porches, roofs, basements, garage conversions, additions and new homes. Painting, new flooring, cabinets, countertops and swapping fixtures in the same spot usually don't need one. Permit fees typically run about $100 to $800 for most remodels and more for additions and new homes, and simple permits can be issued in a few days. Do I need a permit to remodel my house in Gainesville or Hall County?

A simple way to think about it: if the work is behind the walls, under the floor, on the roof or outside the current footprint, plan on a permit. If it's a finish you can see and touch, like paint, flooring, cabinets or a new faucet in the same place, you usually don't need one. Georgia uses the International Residential Code, and the City of Gainesville, Hall County, Flowery Branch, Oakwood and Buford all enforce it, with their own local rules on top.

Who issues building permits in Hall County, GA?

It depends on where your house sits, not your mailing address. A Gainesville address can be in the city or in unincorporated Hall County.

  • Inside Gainesville city limits: City of Gainesville.
  • Unincorporated Hall County (including Murrayville, Chestnut Mountain and much of the Lake Lanier shoreline): Hall County.
  • Flowery Branch, Oakwood and Buford: each handles permits inside its own city limits.
  • Septic systems: Hall County Environmental Health. Adding a bedroom on septic usually means the system's capacity gets reviewed.
  • Lake Lanier docks and shoreline work: the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, separate from your building permit. Our Lake Lanier guide covers that.

Homes north of Gainesville in White, Lumpkin and Habersham counties go through those counties or their cities. Eric checks the jurisdiction for every address before the estimate, so you don't have to figure it out.

How much does a building permit cost?

Most departments base the fee on the project's value or square footage, then add separate trade permits for plumbing, electrical and HVAC. Plan review is charged on bigger jobs. As a rough guide in the Gainesville area:

  • Small jobs like a re-roof, deck or single-room remodel: about $100 to $500.
  • Kitchen and bathroom remodels with trade permits: about $150 to $800.
  • Additions and sunrooms: about $500 to $2,500.
  • New homes: about $2,000 to $10,000 or more once plan review, trade permits and any impact or utility fees are added.

The permit is a small slice of the job. A $25,000 kitchen remodel might carry a few hundred dollars in permit fees, which is cheap insurance next to the cost of tearing out work that doesn't pass.

How long does it take to get a permit?

Typical timing around Gainesville:

  • A few days for simple permits like a re-roof, a deck rebuild or a trade permit.
  • 1 to 3 weeks for remodels that need drawings, such as wall removal or a kitchen layout change.
  • 2 to 6 weeks for additions and new homes, which get a full plan review for setbacks, structure and energy code.

Spring is busiest, so permits can take longer then. We usually submit while you're still choosing finishes, so the permit is in hand by the time materials arrive.

What inspections happen during a remodel?

Inspections follow the order of the work. Not every job gets every one, but a typical remodel or addition sees:

  1. Footing or foundation for decks, porches and additions, before concrete is poured.
  2. Rough-in for framing, plumbing, electrical and HVAC, while the walls are still open.
  3. Insulation before drywall, to check it meets Georgia's energy code.
  4. Final when the work is finished. New homes and some additions also get a certificate of occupancy.

Eric schedules each inspection and meets the inspector, so you don't need to take time off work.

What happens if I remodel without a permit?

Skipping the permit can cost far more than the permit itself:

  • Stop-work orders and fines if the building department finds out mid-project.
  • Opening walls so an inspector can see the wiring, plumbing and framing, then patching them again.
  • Trouble when you sell. Buyers' inspectors and appraisers in Hall County look for additions, finished basements and converted garages that aren't on record, and unpermitted space may not count toward your square footage.
  • Insurance questions. If a fire or leak starts in unpermitted work, your insurer may push back on the claim.
  • Safety. Inspections catch undersized beams, overloaded circuits and missing deck hardware before they fail.

Should I pull the permit myself or let my contractor do it?

Let your contractor do it. Georgia generally lets homeowners pull a permit for their own home, but whoever pulls it takes responsibility for the work meeting code. A contractor who asks you to pull the permit is often unlicensed or trying to avoid accountability. EBD Construction pulls every permit itself, under its own license. What about HOA approval?

A permit and HOA approval are two different things, and many neighborhoods in Flowery Branch, Oakwood, Buford, Braselton and around Lake Lanier require both. HOAs often review exterior work like decks, porches, additions, roofs, siding colors and fences. Building permit FAQs

Do I need a permit to replace my water heater or HVAC system?

Usually, yes. Water heaters and HVAC replacements generally need a plumbing or mechanical permit, even when the new unit goes in the same spot, because gas lines, venting and electrical connections are involved.

Do I need a permit for a shed or fence?

Small sheds, often under 200 square feet, are frequently exempt from a building permit, but zoning setbacks and HOA rules still apply. Fences usually don't need a building permit in Hall County but can have height and placement limits. Check before you build.

Do I need a permit to replace windows or doors?

Swapping a window or door in the same opening often doesn't need one. Making an opening bigger, adding a new one or cutting into a load-bearing wall does.

Does a permit raise my property taxes?

Adding square footage, like an addition or finished basement, can raise your assessed value whether or not it was permitted. Permitted space also counts when you sell, so it usually pays you back.

How long is a building permit good for?

Permits generally expire if work doesn't start or stalls for an extended period, commonly around six months. Keeping a job moving and inspections on schedule keeps the permit active.
