Remodel, Add On or Build New in Gainesville, GA?

An honest comparison of remodeling, adding on, tearing down and building new in Gainesville and Hall County, from a builder who does all four.

By Eric Bubniak, owner of EBD Construction LLC · October 8, 2026

Quick answer: If you like your lot and the house has good bones, remodeling or adding on is usually cheaper and faster than building new. In Gainesville and Hall County, remodels start around $1,500 for a single room, additions around $5,000, and new homes run about $150 to $350 or more per square foot plus land. Building new makes sense when the house no longer fits the way you live, the layout can't be fixed, or you own land you love. When does remodeling make the most sense?

  • You love the location. Your neighborhood, schools, lot or lake access would be hard to replace.
  • The house has good bones. The foundation, framing and roof are sound, even if the finishes are dated.
  • The problems are fixable. A closed-in kitchen, a dated bath or a small living room can be solved by opening a wall or reworking a room.
  • You want to phase the work. Remodeling can be done one room at a time as budget allows.

When does adding on make the most sense?

An addition fits when you like the house but simply need more room: a bigger kitchen, a primary suite on the main floor, a bedroom for a growing family or space for aging parents. If your house has an unfinished basement, finishing it is usually the least expensive way to add space. If not, a bump-out, sunroom or full room addition can be built to match the existing house.

When does building a new home make the most sense?

  • You own land or found the right lot. Family land in north Hall, a lake lot or acreage you love. See building north of Gainesville.
  • The layout can't be fixed. When almost every wall would move, remodeling can cost close to building new.
  • Major systems are failing. Foundation problems, widespread rot or a house that needs everything replaced at once.
  • You want something specific. One-level living, a walk-out basement, a big porch or energy efficiency built in from day one.

Should I tear down and rebuild?

A knock-down rebuild can make sense on a great lot with a house that isn't worth saving, which happens in older Gainesville neighborhoods and on Lake Lanier lots with small original cabins. You keep the location and existing utilities, and you get a brand-new house. Budget for demolition, typically about $8,000 to $30,000 depending on size and access, plus testing for asbestos or lead in older homes. Eric can look at the house and give you an honest opinion on whether it's worth remodeling.

How do I pay for a remodel, addition or new home?

  • Cash or savings for smaller remodels and phased work.
  • A home equity loan or HELOC for remodels and additions, borrowing against the home you already own.
  • A cash-out refinance for larger renovations.
  • A renovation loan that's based on the home's value after the work.
  • A construction loan for a new home or a rebuild, often converting to a regular mortgage when the house is finished.

Talk with your lender early. A written estimate from EBD helps them size the loan.

How do I decide which option is right for me?

  1. Write down what isn't working about your current house, and what you'd keep.
  2. Set a comfortable budget range, including a cushion for surprises.
  3. Get the house looked at. A contractor can tell you whether the structure, roof and systems are worth building on.
  4. Compare two or three options side by side, with cost and timeline for each.
  5. Think about how long you'll stay. The longer you'll live there, the more a bigger change pays off.

Remodel or build new FAQs

Is it cheaper to remodel or build new in Hall County?

Usually remodeling is cheaper, because the foundation, roof, walls and utilities already exist. Building new can come close in cost when nearly every wall would have to move or major systems are failing.

Does a remodel or a new home add more value?

A new home is worth more, but it also costs more. Kitchens, baths, porches and finished space usually return a good share of their cost and make the home easier to sell.

Can I remodel one room at a time?

Yes. Many homeowners phase the work, starting with the kitchen or primary bath, and EBD can design each phase to fit with the next.
