How a screened porch, a three-season room and a sunroom compare on cost, comfort, pollen and year-round use in Gainesville and Hall County.

The short version: a screened porch brings you outside without the mosquitoes; a sunroom brings the outside in, with walls, windows and air conditioning. A three-season room is the in-between option, with windows you can open, built for spring and fall.

Is a screened porch or a sunroom better in Georgia?

For many Gainesville homeowners, a screened porch is the better value. North Georgia weather allows outdoor living for most of the year, and a screened porch with a ceiling fan is comfortable from early spring into late fall. It costs less, keeps out mosquitoes and gnats, and still feels like being outside, which is the point for a lot of lake and wooded lots.

A sunroom is the better choice if:

  • You want a room you'll use every day of the year, including July afternoons and January mornings.
  • Spring pollen keeps you indoors. Screens don't stop the yellow pine pollen that coats everything in Hall County each spring.
  • You want the space to work as a home office, playroom or dining area, not just an outdoor sitting area.
  • You'd like the room to count as finished living space.

Not sure? A three-season room, or a screened porch built so it can be enclosed later, keeps both doors open.

Can I turn my screened porch into a sunroom later?

Often, yes. If the porch roof, framing and foundation are in good shape, the screens can be replaced with insulated walls and windows, and heating and cooling can be added. The floor usually needs insulation, and the roof may need insulation and a new ceiling. Eric will check the existing structure during the consultation and tell you whether a conversion makes sense or whether starting fresh costs less in the long run.

Do I need a permit for a screened porch or sunroom in Gainesville or Hall County?

Usually, yes. A new porch, deck, roof or sunroom needs a building permit and inspections, and turning a porch into heated and cooled space typically does too. Simply replacing old screen on an existing porch usually does not. Homes inside Gainesville city limits are permitted through the City of Gainesville; homes in unincorporated Hall County go through Hall County. Flowery Branch, Oakwood and Buford each have their own process. EBD Construction pulls all required permits itself as part of the project, so you don't have to.

Screened porch and sunroom FAQs

Does a sunroom add more value than a screened porch?

A sunroom that is heated and cooled can count as finished living space, while a screened porch usually doesn't. A well-built screened porch is still one of the most requested features in Gainesville and Lake Lanier homes, and it costs less to build.

Can a screened porch keep out pollen?

Standard screen keeps out bugs and leaves but not the fine yellow pine pollen Gainesville gets every spring. If pollen is the main problem, a three-season room or sunroom with windows is the better fit.

Can I add windows to a screened porch for the winter?

Yes. Removable or sliding vinyl window panels can turn a screened porch into a three-season room for cooler months, at less cost than a full sunroom.

Can I put a fireplace or TV on a screened porch?

Yes. Screened porches with ceiling fans, recessed lights, outdoor TVs and fireplaces are some of the most popular projects in Hall County. Plan the wiring and gas lines before the ceiling goes on.

Can you screen in the area under my deck?

Often, yes. The space under a second-story deck can become a screened patio if the deck above is waterproofed or fitted with an under-deck drainage system.

What kind of ceiling is best for a porch or sunroom?

Tongue-and-groove pine or cedar ceilings are a favorite in the Gainesville area. They hold up well, take a stain or paint, and hide fan and light wiring cleanly.
