How Do You Pay for a Home Remodel in Gainesville, GA?

The common ways to pay for a kitchen, bath, addition or whole-home remodel, how contractor payment schedules work, and how to make a smaller budget go further, for homeowners in Gainesville, Hall County and around Lake Lanier.

By Eric Bubniak, owner of EBD Construction LLC · October 30, 2026

Quick answer: Most homeowners in Gainesville and Hall County pay for a remodel with savings, a home equity line of credit (HELOC), a fixed-rate home equity loan, a cash-out refinance, or, when buying a fixer-upper, a renovation mortgage. Borrowing against your home usually costs less than personal loans or credit cards. Start with a written estimate so you know how much to borrow, add a 10 to 20 percent contingency, and ask for the payment schedule in writing. How much can I borrow against my home?

Most lenders let you borrow up to about 80 to 90 percent of your home's value, minus what you still owe. For example, on a home worth $400,000 with a $250,000 mortgage balance, 85 percent of the value is $340,000, leaving up to about $90,000 available. Home values across Hall County have risen a lot in recent years, so many homeowners have more equity than they think. Your lender will order an appraisal or use an automated value to confirm it.

How do contractor payment schedules work?

A remodeling contract usually spreads payments across the job so you're never far ahead of the work. A typical schedule looks like this:

  1. Deposit at signing. Reserves your spot on the schedule and covers ordering materials such as cabinets, windows or trusses.
  2. Progress payments at milestones. For example, after framing or rough-in inspections, after cabinets are set, or after drywall.
  3. Final payment after the walkthrough. Once the work is finished, inspected and any punch-list items are done.

Get the schedule in writing with your estimate, tie payments to finished work rather than dates, and be wary of anyone asking for most of the money up front. Renovation loans and some HELOCs pay out in draws after an inspection, so tell your contractor early if your lender works that way. How can I make my budget go further?

  • Keep the layout. Leaving sinks, toilets and appliances where they are saves thousands in plumbing and electrical.
  • Refresh what's solid. Paint or reface good cabinets instead of replacing them. See our cabinet guide.
  • Phase the project. Do structural and behind-the-walls work first, and add finishes, a second bathroom or a deck later.
  • Spend where it counts. Kitchens, baths and curb appeal return the most when you sell. See our remodeling ROI guide.
  • Schedule smart. Interior work in the winter months can be easier to schedule. See our best time to remodel guide.
  • Decide before you start. Changes after work begins are the biggest budget killer. Seeing AI mockups of your own space first helps; see how AI remodel mockups work.

Are there tax breaks for remodeling?

Sometimes. Interest on a home equity loan or HELOC may be tax-deductible when the money is used to buy, build or substantially improve the home that secures the loan, within IRS limits, and only if you itemize. Energy upgrades and medically necessary changes, such as some aging-in-place work, can have their own rules. Keep your contracts and receipts, because improvements also add to your home's cost basis when you sell. Ask a tax professional what applies to you. If you're planning accessibility changes, see our aging-in-place guide.

Paying for a remodel FAQs

What is a HELOC and how does it work for a remodel?

A home equity line of credit lets you borrow against your home's equity as you need it, usually with a variable rate and interest-only payments during a draw period of about 10 years. It works well for remodels because you draw money as the contractor's payments come due instead of borrowing it all at once.

Is a HELOC or a cash-out refinance better for a remodel?

If your current mortgage rate is lower than today's rates, a HELOC or home equity loan usually makes more sense, because it leaves your first mortgage alone. A cash-out refinance can make sense if current rates are close to or below your existing rate, or for a very large project.

How much equity do I need to borrow for a remodel?

Many lenders let you borrow up to about 80 to 90 percent of your home's value, minus what you owe on your mortgage. On a $400,000 home with a $250,000 balance, 85 percent would allow up to about $90,000. Each lender sets its own limits.

How do contractor payments work for a remodel?

Most remodeling contracts use a deposit to schedule the work and order materials, then progress payments tied to milestones such as rough-in inspections, cabinet installation or drywall, and a final payment after the walkthrough. Ask for the payment schedule in writing with the estimate.

Can I do my remodel in phases?

Yes. Phasing is a good way to stay within budget. Do the work that has to happen first, such as structural, plumbing and electrical, and save finishes or a second room for later. A good contractor will plan the first phase so it doesn't have to be torn up for the next one.

Can a renovation loan be used to buy and fix up a house?

Yes. Renovation mortgages such as the FHA 203(k) and Fannie Mae HomeStyle loans roll the purchase price and the remodel into one mortgage, based on the home's value after the work. They require detailed contractor bids and inspections during the job.
