How Much Does Water Damage Repair Cost in Gainesville, GA?

What to do in the first 48 hours, what it costs to rebuild drywall, ceilings, subfloors, floors, kitchens and bathrooms after a leak, and how insurance usually works, for homeowners in Gainesville, Hall County and around Lake Lanier.

By Eric Bubniak, owner of EBD Construction LLC · November 13, 2026

Quick answer: Once the water is dried out, rebuilding typically costs about $500 to $3,000 for drywall or a ceiling in one room, $800 to $4,000 for a section of subfloor, $5,000 to $30,000 to rebuild a bathroom, and $8,000 to $40,000 to rebuild a kitchen after a sink or dishwasher leak. A whole floor after a burst pipe can run $25,000 to $80,000+. Insurance usually covers sudden leaks, minus your deductible. What should I do in the first 48 hours?

  1. Stop the water. Close the valve at the fixture or the main shutoff, usually near the water heater, in the crawl space or at the meter.
  2. Make it safe. Turn off breakers to wet rooms if you can reach the panel without standing in water.
  3. Take photos and video of everything wet before anything is moved or thrown out.
  4. Call your insurance company and start a claim if the leak was sudden.
  5. Get it dried. A water mitigation company pulls out water, wet drywall, carpet and pad, and sets fans and dehumidifiers. Wood and drywall need to be dry within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold.
  6. Then plan the rebuild. Once moisture readings are back to normal, we can open up, inspect and price the repair.

What commonly causes water damage around Gainesville?

  • Frozen pipes in crawl spaces and exterior walls during January cold snaps.
  • Water heaters that leak or burst after 10 to 15 years.
  • Washing machine hoses, ice maker lines, toilet supply lines and dishwasher connections.
  • Failed shower pans and cracked grout that leak slowly into the subfloor.
  • Roof and flashing leaks after storms. See our roof replacement guide.
  • Gutters and grading that send water into crawl spaces and basements.

How does insurance usually work?

Most homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, like a burst pipe, a failed water heater or a broken supply line, minus your deductible. They usually don't cover slow leaks over time, rot or mold from long-term moisture, or flooding from rising water, which needs a separate flood policy. The adjuster writes up the damage; we give you a detailed, written rebuild estimate you can share with them. You choose your own contractor. Check your policy or ask your agent for the details of your coverage.

Can I upgrade while it's torn apart?

Yes, and it's often the best time. If the kitchen base cabinets have to come out anyway, many homeowners pay the difference for new countertops, a new layout or an island. If one room of flooring is ruined, running new floor through the whole main level avoids a patchwork. We price the like-for-like repair and the upgrade separately. What about mold?

Mold can start on wet drywall, wood and carpet in 24 to 48 hours. If you see or smell mold, or water sat for more than a couple of days, a mold remediation company should test and clean it before the rebuild. Remediation typically costs about $1,500 to $8,000 for a contained area. We don't close up walls until the framing is dry and clean.

Do I need a permit?

Replacing drywall, flooring and cabinets like-for-like usually doesn't need a permit. Replacing wiring, plumbing, a water heater, structural framing or moving walls during the rebuild usually does. Eric pulls all permits himself; see our building permits guide.

Water damage repair FAQs

What should I do first after water damage?

Stop the water at the fixture or the main shutoff, turn off power to wet areas if it's safe, take photos and video, and call your insurance company. Then have a water mitigation company remove the water and dry the house with fans and dehumidifiers within 24 to 48 hours, before mold starts. Rebuilding comes after everything is dry.

Does homeowners insurance cover water damage?

Most policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, like a burst pipe, a failed water heater or a broken supply line, minus your deductible. They usually don't cover slow leaks over time, lack of maintenance, or flooding from rising water, which needs a separate flood policy. Check your policy or ask your agent.

What is the difference between water mitigation and water damage repair?

Mitigation is the emergency step: pumping out water, removing wet drywall, carpet and pad, and drying the structure. Repair, or the rebuild, is putting the house back: new drywall, insulation, subfloor, flooring, cabinets, trim and paint. EBD Construction does the rebuild after the house has been dried.

How long does it take to repair water damage?

Drying usually takes 3 to 5 days. A rebuild of drywall and flooring in a room or two typically takes 1 to 3 weeks. A kitchen or bathroom rebuild, or a whole floor after a burst pipe, usually takes 4 to 10 weeks, often depending on cabinet and material lead times.

Can I use the rebuild to upgrade my kitchen or bathroom?

Yes. Many homeowners use the insurance payment for the damaged parts and pay the difference to upgrade, like quartz counters, a tiled walk-in shower or new flooring through the whole level. We price the like-for-like repair and the upgrade separately so it's clear.

When is mold a concern?

Mold can start growing on wet drywall, wood and carpet in 24 to 48 hours. If you see or smell mold, or water sat for more than a couple of days, a mold remediation company should test and clean it before the rebuild. Remediation typically costs about $1,500 to $8,000 for a contained area.
