What drives the cost of built-ins?

  • Size. Cost tracks linear feet and height; going to the ceiling costs more but looks the most built-in.
  • Doors and drawers vs. open shelves. Drawers and doors cost more than shelves; most designs mix closed storage below with open shelves above.
  • Paint-grade or stain-grade. Painted MDF and poplar is the most common. Stained white oak or walnut costs about 20 to 50 percent more.
  • Details. Crown molding, beadboard or shiplap backs, glass doors, and brass or black hardware.
  • Lighting and power. LED shelf lights, outlets for a TV or desk, and charging drawers add electrical work.
  • Odd spaces. Sloped ceilings in bonus rooms and under stairs take more fitting, but that's where built-ins shine.

Where do built-ins make the most sense?

Around a fireplace or TV wall in the living room, in a home office, as a window seat in a bedroom or bay window, under the eaves in a finished bonus room, as a drop zone by the garage door, under the stairs, and in a dining room as a buffet or china cabinet. In kitchens, a floor-to-ceiling pantry wall is a built-in too; see what a pantry costs.

Custom, semi-custom or ready-made boxes?

Fully custom built-ins are built to fit exactly, including uneven walls and sloped ceilings. A hybrid uses semi-custom cabinet boxes for the base and site-built shelves, face frames and trim above, which often saves 15 to 30 percent and still reads as built-in. Store-bought furniture pieces trimmed in can work for a tight budget but rarely look as finished. We'll price the options side by side.

Cabinets and shelves usually don't need a permit in Gainesville or Hall County. Custom built-in FAQs

Are custom built-ins worth it?

Built-ins add storage without taking floor space, make a room look finished, and are one of the details buyers notice in a living room, office or mudroom. They cost more than freestanding furniture but last as long as the house, and they can fit odd spaces like sloped ceilings, alcoves and under-stair areas that furniture can't.

What is the difference between paint-grade and stain-grade built-ins?

Paint-grade built-ins are made from MDF and poplar or birch plywood and painted, which hides joints and is the most common and affordable choice. Stain-grade built-ins use hardwood plywood and solid wood like white oak, walnut or cherry with a clear or stained finish. They cost about 20 to 50 percent more because the wood and joinery are exposed.

Are built-ins cheaper than semi-custom cabinets?

For a simple wall, using semi-custom base and wall cabinets topped with site-built shelves and trim can cost less than fully custom work and still look built-in. Fully custom built-ins cost more but fit exactly, including sloped ceilings, uneven walls and odd sizes. We often combine the two to fit the budget.

How long does it take to build custom built-ins?

Design and ordering take about 1 to 3 weeks. Installation usually takes 3 days to 1 week for a window seat or single wall and 1 to 3 weeks for a fireplace wall, media wall or office, including painting or finishing. If ready-made cabinet boxes are used, their lead time is usually 2 to 8 weeks.

Do built-ins need a permit?

Built-in cabinets and shelves usually don't need a permit in Gainesville or Hall County. Adding new outlets, lighting circuits or a wall-mounted TV with in-wall wiring may need an electrical permit, and work near a fireplace has to meet code clearances. Eric Bubniak pulls any permits himself.

Can built-ins be added to an existing home?

Yes. Most built-ins we install go into existing homes, around fireplaces, in bonus rooms, under stairs, in closets and in mudrooms. We scribe the cabinets to uneven walls and floors, run trim to match the house, and can add lighting and outlets.
