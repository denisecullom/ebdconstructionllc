How Much Does an In-Law Suite Cost in Gainesville, GA?

Ways to create a private suite for a parent, adult child or long-term guest, what each one costs, how long it takes and what Hall County zoning allows, for homeowners in Gainesville, Flowery Branch, Oakwood and around Lake Lanier.

By Eric Bubniak, owner of EBD Construction LLC · November 4, 2026

Quick answer: An in-law suite in Gainesville and Hall County typically costs about $15,000 to $50,000 to turn existing rooms into a suite, $30,000 to $100,000+ for a garage conversion, $35,000 to $120,000+ for a basement suite, $50,000 to $180,000 for an attached addition, and $90,000 to $250,000+ for a detached guest cottage. A kitchenette adds about $5,000 to $20,000 and an accessible bathroom about $8,000 to $35,000. What are the ways to add an in-law suite?

An in-law suite is a private bedroom, bathroom and living area, often with a kitchenette and its own entrance, for a parent, adult child or long-term guest. There are five common ways to create one:

Convert existing roomsTurn a bedroom wing or bonus room into a suite with an updated bath$15,000–$50,0003–8 weeks
Garage conversionBedroom, bath and living area inside an attached garage$30,000–$100,000+8–12 weeks
Basement suiteFinish a walk-out basement with bedroom, bath and kitchenette$35,000–$120,000+6–12 weeks
Attached additionA new wing with its own bedroom, bath and sitting room$50,000–$180,0003–6 months

Plan on 2 to 6 weeks for design and permits before work starts. Additions run about $75 to $300 per square foot, depending on how much plumbing, roofing and finish work is involved.

Which option is right for my family?

  • Tight budget, room to spare: convert a bedroom wing or bonus room you aren't using. See our bonus room guide.
  • Walk-out basement: often the best value, with its own entrance and natural light.
  • Attached garage you can give up: a quick, contained project, though you lose parking and storage.
  • A parent who can't do stairs: a main-level addition or garage conversion, built for aging in place.
  • Most privacy: a detached cottage, if your lot and zoning allow it. Larger rural lots in north and east Hall County often do.

What does Hall County zoning allow?

Rules for in-law suites and accessory dwellings vary by zoning district in unincorporated Hall County, the City of Gainesville, Oakwood, Flowery Branch and nearby towns. The common questions are whether you can have a second full kitchen, a separate entrance, a separate address or electric meter, a detached unit, and whether the space can ever be rented. Neighborhood covenants and HOA rules can add their own limits. Eric checks your property's zoning and any HOA rules before design, so the plan you fall in love with is one that can be permitted. He pulls all permits himself; see our building permits guide.

How should an in-law suite be designed?

  • Plan for aging in place. A no-step entry, 36-inch doors, a curbless shower, grab-bar blocking and lever handles. See our aging-in-place guide.
  • Give it its own entrance plus a door into the main house, so it works for privacy and for help when needed.
  • Separate the noise. Insulated walls and a solid door between the suite and the main living area make a big difference.
  • Give it its own heating and cooling. A ductless mini-split lets each household set its own temperature.
  • Think about later. Design the suite so it can become a guest room, home office or rental-ready space down the road, if zoning allows.

In-law suite FAQs

What is the cheapest way to add an in-law suite?

Converting space you already have under roof is usually cheapest: a bedroom and bath at one end of the house, a basement, or a garage. The foundation, roof and walls already exist, so you mostly pay for finishes, plumbing and electrical. An addition or detached cottage costs more because you build the whole structure.

Can I put a kitchen in an in-law suite in Hall County?

Often you can add a kitchenette, but rules on a second full kitchen, a separate entrance, a separate address or meter, and renting the space vary by zoning district in Hall County, the City of Gainesville and nearby towns. Eric checks your property's zoning before design so the plan can be permitted.

How long does it take to build an in-law suite?

A conversion of existing rooms takes about 3 to 8 weeks of work. Garage and basement suites take about 6 to 12 weeks. An attached addition usually takes 3 to 6 months, and a detached cottage 4 to 8 months, plus 2 to 6 weeks for design and permits.

Does an in-law suite add value to my home?

A well-built, permitted suite with its own bath usually adds value and appeals to multigenerational buyers, which are common around Gainesville. It rarely returns every dollar at resale, so the main payoff is the years of use. Unpermitted suites can hurt a sale.

Should an in-law suite be accessible?

If it's for a parent, plan for aging in place from the start: a no-step entry, 36-inch doors, a curbless or low-threshold shower, grab-bar blocking and lever handles. These cost far less to build in than to add later.

Do I need a permit for an in-law suite?

Yes. An addition, garage or basement conversion, new bathroom or kitchenette needs building, electrical and plumbing permits, and a new detached unit may need zoning review and septic approval. Eric pulls all permits himself.
