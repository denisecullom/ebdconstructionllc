What Does It Cost to Remodel an Older Ranch or Split-Level Home in Gainesville, GA?

What to check for, what it costs to fix and which updates make the biggest difference in 1950s to 1980s homes across Gainesville, Hall County and the Lake Lanier area.

By Eric Bubniak, owner of EBD Construction LLC · October 24, 2026

Quick answer: Remodeling an older ranch or split-level in Gainesville and Hall County usually costs about $7,000 to $50,000 for cosmetic updates, $15,000 to $120,000 for a main-level remodel with a new kitchen and open floor plan, and $40,000 to $250,000+ for a full renovation. Homes built from the 1950s to the 1980s often also need behind-the-walls updates, such as a panel upgrade ($2,000–$5,000), rewiring ($8,000–$25,000) or repiping ($4,000–$15,000), so plan on a 10 to 20 percent contingency. Why remodel an older ranch or split-level?

Gainesville and Hall County are full of brick ranches and split-levels built from the 1950s through the 1980s, in established neighborhoods close to town and the lake. They often sit on bigger, wooded lots than new subdivisions, and they're built simply, with straight rooflines and easy-to-reach crawl spaces or basements. That makes them great candidates for a remodel. The usual complaints are the same ones in almost every house: a closed-off kitchen, small dated bathrooms, low or dark rooms, and wiring and plumbing that are 40 to 70 years old.

Which updates make the biggest difference in an older home?

  • Open the kitchen. Taking out the wall between the kitchen and the living or dining room is the most popular change in older ranches. It often means a new beam, typically $3,000 to $15,000. See our wall removal guide.
  • A new kitchen. Original kitchens in these homes are small and closed in. See our kitchen remodeling cost guide, or our cabinet guide if the boxes are still solid.
  • Updated bathrooms. Most have one small hall bath and a tiny primary bath. Swapping a tub for a walk-in shower or enlarging the primary bath makes a big difference. See our tub-to-shower conversion guide.
  • One floor throughout. Pulling up old carpet, vinyl and parquet and running one floor through the main level makes the house feel larger. Many ranches have oak hardwood under the carpet that can be refinished. See our flooring guide.
  • A vaulted ceiling. In homes framed with rafters rather than trusses, raising a flat ceiling in the living room can be possible, typically $10,000 to $40,000 with a structural beam.
  • Curb appeal. New siding or paint, a covered front porch, new shutters and a new front door turn a plain ranch into a standout. See our exterior remodeling guide.

What about lead paint and asbestos?

Both are common in homes from this era, and both are manageable. Lead paint: if your home was built before 1978, federal EPA rules require renovation work that disturbs painted surfaces to be done by a certified firm using lead-safe practices, such as plastic containment, no dry sanding and careful cleanup. A lead test before work starts shows where it is. Asbestos: popcorn ceilings, old 9-by-9-inch floor tiles and their black glue, and some pipe and duct insulation can contain it. It's only a hazard when disturbed, so suspect materials should be tested before demolition and, if positive, removed by a licensed abatement company. Ask any contractor you're considering how they handle both.

Do I need permits to remodel an older home?

Most of the work in an older-home remodel needs permits: rewiring, panel upgrades, repiping, moving plumbing, removing load-bearing walls and additions. Inside city limits that's the City of Gainesville, and in unincorporated areas it's Hall County. Older homes sometimes have earlier work done without permits, which may need to be brought up to code during the remodel. Eric pulls every permit himself. See our building permits guide.

Older home remodeling FAQs

What problems should I look for in a 1960s or 1970s house?

The most common are an undersized or recalled electrical panel, two-prong ungrounded outlets, aluminum branch wiring in some homes built around 1965 to 1973, galvanized steel or polybutylene water pipes, aging cast iron drains, possible asbestos in popcorn ceilings and old floor tiles, lead paint in homes built before 1978, and moisture or sagging floors in crawl spaces.

Is it worth remodeling an old ranch house?

Often yes. Many 1950s to 1980s ranches in Gainesville and Hall County sit on large, established lots in convenient neighborhoods and have simple, sturdy framing that is easy to update. Opening the kitchen, updating baths and floors and bringing the systems up to date can make one feel like a new home for less than building new.

Can you open up a ranch house floor plan?

Usually. Many ranches have a load-bearing wall down the middle, so removing walls between the kitchen, dining and living rooms often needs a new beam, but it is very common. Load-bearing wall removal with a beam typically costs about $3,000 to $15,000.

Do older homes have asbestos?

Homes built before about 1980 may contain asbestos in popcorn ceilings, 9-by-9-inch vinyl floor tiles and their glue, pipe and duct insulation, and some siding. Have suspect materials tested before they are disturbed; testing usually costs about $250 to $750, and removal by a licensed abatement company often $1,500 to $7,000 or more.

What should I know about lead paint in an older home?

Homes built before 1978 may have lead paint. Federal EPA rules require renovation work that disturbs painted surfaces in those homes to be done by a certified firm using lead-safe practices, such as containment and careful cleanup. A lead test before work starts tells you where it is.

How much should I budget for surprises in an older home?

Plan on a contingency of 10 to 20 percent of the project cost. Rot under a bathroom floor, old wiring inside walls and crawl space repairs are the most common surprises in Gainesville-area homes from the 1950s to 1980s.
