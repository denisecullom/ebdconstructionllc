What does a residential general contractor do?

A general contractor (GC) is the one company responsible for your whole project. On a home addition or major remodel that means:

  • Estimate and contract. A written, line-by-line price, a scope of work and a payment schedule tied to progress.
  • Permits and inspections. Drawing the permit with the City of Gainesville or Hall County and meeting the inspector at footing, framing, rough-in and final.
  • Scheduling the trades. Framing, plumbing, electrical, HVAC, insulation, drywall, tile, cabinets, paint and flooring in the right order.
  • Materials. Ordering long-lead items like windows, cabinets and trusses early so the job doesn't stall.
  • Quality and problem solving. Catching rot, bad wiring or out-of-square walls when they're found, and pricing the fix before going further.
  • Cleanup and closeout. Keeping the site safe and clean, finishing the punch list, closing the permit and standing behind the work.

When does Georgia require a licensed general contractor?

Georgia requires a residential or general contractor license from the State Licensing Board for Residential and General Contractors for most residential construction work valued over $2,500. A residential license covers one- and two-family homes and townhomes; a general contractor license covers residential and commercial work. Plumbers, electricians and HVAC contractors hold their own separate state licenses. Before you hire anyone, look up their license on the Georgia Secretary of State's online license lookup, ask for a certificate of insurance, and confirm they will pull the permit. How do payments work with a general contractor?

A sound contract sets a modest deposit to get on the schedule and order materials, then ties the remaining payments to finished milestones, such as foundation, framing and dry-in, rough-ins, drywall, and completion, with the final payment after the walkthrough. Be wary of anyone who asks for most of the money up front or wants cash with no written contract. Changes should be priced and agreed in writing before the work is done.

Permits and inspections in Gainesville and Hall County

Inside the City of Gainesville, permits come from the city; in unincorporated Hall County they come from Hall County's building department; and Flowery Branch, Oakwood and the other towns have their own offices. New homes and additions need plan review and inspections at footing, foundation, framing, rough plumbing, electrical and mechanical, insulation and final. Homes on septic may also need Health Department approval, and lake lots on Lake Lanier may need Corps of Engineers sign-off for shoreline work. Eric pulls every permit himself. What does a general contractor do?

A residential general contractor takes responsibility for the whole project: estimating, the contract, permits and inspections, scheduling and supervising carpenters and subcontractors such as plumbers, electricians, HVAC and drywall, ordering materials, keeping the job safe and clean, and closing out the final inspection.

Do I need a licensed contractor in Georgia?

Yes for most projects. Georgia requires a residential or general contractor license from the State Licensing Board for Residential and General Contractors for most residential construction work valued over $2,500. You can check any license on the Georgia Secretary of State's online license lookup.

How much does a general contractor charge?

Most residential general contractors include their overhead and profit in a fixed project price rather than billing it separately. Around Gainesville, typical project costs are about $15,000 to $90,000 for a single room addition, $30,000 to $160,000 for a primary suite addition, $40,000 to $250,000 for a full whole-home renovation, and $150 to $350 or more per square foot for a new custom home.

Should I hire a general contractor or the trades myself?

Hiring the trades yourself can save the contractor's margin on small, single-trade jobs, but you take on the permits, scheduling, inspections and the risk if one trade's work holds up another. For anything that touches structure or two or more trades, such as a kitchen, bathroom, addition or new home, a general contractor usually saves time and mistakes.

Who pulls the permit, the homeowner or the contractor?

The licensed contractor should pull the permit. If a contractor asks you to pull it as the owner, they may not be licensed, and you become responsible for the work passing inspection. Eric Bubniak pulls all of EBD's permits himself.
