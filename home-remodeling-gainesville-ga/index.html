How do you choose a remodeler in Gainesville?

Check five things before you sign: a Georgia residential or general contractor license (required for most residential work over $2,500), proof of liability insurance, who pulls the permits, a detailed written estimate and contract with a payment schedule tied to progress, and local references or finished projects you can see. Ask who will actually be in your house every day. Our full checklist is in how to choose a remodeling contractor in Gainesville.

Remodel, add on or build new?

If the house is in the right spot and structurally sound, remodeling or adding on is usually cheaper than moving or building, once you count closing costs and moving. If the layout fights you everywhere and the systems are worn out, building new can make more sense. We walk through the math in remodel or build new, and cover which projects pay back at resale in which remodels add the most value. For budgeting and timing, see the best time of year to remodel.

How long does a home remodel take?

A bathroom usually takes 3 to 5 weeks, a kitchen 6 to 12 weeks, a basement 4 to 10 weeks, a room addition 3 to 5 months, and a whole-home renovation 2 to 8 months depending on scope. Design, selections and permits usually add 2 to 6 weeks before work starts.

Do I need a permit to remodel my home in Gainesville or Hall County?

Most remodels that change plumbing, electrical, HVAC, structure or square footage need a permit from the City of Gainesville or Hall County, depending on where the house is. Cosmetic work like paint, cabinets and flooring usually doesn't. EBD's owner Eric Bubniak pulls all permits himself.

Which home remodels add the most value?

Smaller, well-done projects usually return the highest share of their cost at resale: a minor kitchen refresh, new siding or a new garage or entry door, and fresh flooring. Large primary suite additions and high-end kitchens add the most enjoyment but return a smaller share of their cost.
